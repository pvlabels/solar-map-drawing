## Callouts

This is the shared source for equipment/array arrow style, routing, spacing and crossing gaps. Scene-format and zone references link here instead of repeating these rules.

Keep wrapped callout lines in one editable text block as defined under [Typography](drawing-rules-production.md#typography). Apply [Typography](drawing-rules-production.md#typography) and [Production process and colors](drawing-rules-production.md#production-process-and-colors) before measuring labels. Preserve source-required wording, identifiers and intentional hierarchy, except for the standardized array identification phrases below. Plain `labels` carry map notes without leaders. Map-only excludes all labels and leaders; TFS retains its supplied-label-only exception.

**Required coverage:** identify source-confirmed arrays plus streets, driveways, pools, sheds, barns, garages, fences and patios, and customer-specified landmarks such as lakes, wells or septic tanks. Electrical-device coverage is defined in [Equipment](drawing-rules-production.md#equipment). Do not invent absent features. Add **YOU ARE HERE only when present in customer artwork**, preserving its confirmed location. Do not add routine callouts for junction boxes, conduit, property lines, temporary objects, chimneys, AC units or skylights; retain an explicit customer-required operational note or necessary obstruction instead of deleting meaningful information.

- Use canonical **Illustrator Arrow 2 at 100% on 1 pt**, from `assets/leader-arrow-2.json`, with the shaft ending inside the sharp head. **Equipment arrows remain one continuous editable stroke with an attached arrowhead.** Never expand the stroke or detach the head as a separate artwork object. SVG uses `marker-end` and its canonical marker definition; in native Illustrator, use the Stroke-panel Arrow 2 setting and verify it remains attached after import. SVG markers do not guarantee native Illustrator Stroke-panel settings.
- **Equipment leaders take the shortest clear local route with only necessary bends:** straight when possible, one elbow when needed, and additional elbows only to clear an actual obstruction. Place the label near its equipment on the same side of the house. Do not send a leader across the house, through multiple rooms or around a long perimeter detour; move the label closer instead. For an interior device, allow only the necessary short final approach into its area. Remove redundant elbows, reversals and outdated `via` points. The renderer tries simpler routes first and rejects unresolved through-house routes; reposition the label and rerender. Keep equipment and map geometry fixed.
- Put other labels close to their targets with short routes. Stagger local groups around the map; do not refit geometry or change weights to shorten leaders. Use horizontal/vertical segments, including the terminal approach. Permit an exact 45-degree segment only when helpful and explicitly `allow_45: true`; no odd angles.
- At a left/right text attachment, leave the normal 4 pt text gap plus a visible horizontal tail before the 90-degree turn. Aim for 6-10 pt of tail; the renderer enforces at least 6 pt. A straight vertical leader is valid only above/below the actual text-block center. Adjust label position to avoid needless jogs.
- Align nearby equipment text to a common local margin, consistent type size and **6 pt clear space between whole text blocks**, including multiline labels. Use `group` and `callout_layout` for this; separate groups on different sides keep routes compact. Reroute old explicit `via` points when labels move.
- **Arrowheads never touch targets.** Start with at least 3 pt target setback and ensure the whole head clears actual target/nearby strokes by 2 pt. A tip must identify its intended device, not an intervening wall. Choose another approach when blocked; do not pull the head back past an unrelated edge. Review crowded targets at high zoom and reading size.
- Keep **2 pt visible clearance between entire arrowheads and between a head and any other leader**. Separate approaches or deliberately stagger heads where they still identify their own targets. Never shrink Arrow 2, move equipment or accept merged heads to force a fit. Keep **6 pt of visible straight shaft behind the rearmost head wing** before any elbow; no bend may sit inside a head. Remove tiny routing jogs (under 4 pt between bends). The renderer tries bounded alternatives and rejects unresolved head collisions or short terminal runs; reroute labels/leaders and review again.
- An array label outside its actual panel/array footprint **must have a leader and Arrow 2 pointing to that specific array**. A nearby floating label or an arrow stopped at the house perimeter is insufficient. Use `array_element` to bind the intended `solar`/`solar_field` target; choose a clear approach to the array, with the usual target gap. Text wholly inside its own array can omit a leader. Preserve TFS's supplied-label-only exception.
- **At every arrow/leader crossing, put an opaque backing box or stroke behind its shaft so the crossed line has a visible gap.** Use white for laser placards and metal, and the matching red background for printed red labels, as specified in [Production process and colors](drawing-rules-production.md#production-process-and-colors). Backing boxes have fill and no outline; the renderer uses an editable **5 pt backing stroke behind the 1 pt shaft**. Place each backing immediately below its own leader and above the crossed geometry, including earlier leaders. Preserve underlying paths. This applies even to legacy `clearance: false`; never substitute a break in the foreground arrow. Keep the route clear of unrelated equipment/text and preserve all head/target clearances.

## Solar-array callout wording

Only these two exact uppercase phrases are allowed for solar-array identification callouts:

- **SOLAR ARRAY ON ROOFTOP** — for a source-confirmed rooftop array.
- **GROUND MOUNT SOLAR ARRAY** — for a source-confirmed ground-mounted array.

Keep the wording exactly as written, including singular `ARRAY`. Line breaks may wrap the phrase without changing its words or order. Do not use abbreviations, plural variants, alternate descriptions, or append extra wording to these callouts. The same wording applies to an identification label placed inside its array without a leader. Separate arrays may repeat the applicable phrase with their own target-bound leaders.

This rule overrides source/customer variations in array identification wording. Retain source-required operational warnings, existing/new distinctions, identifiers and shutdown notes separately as map notes or a legend; they do not create a third array-callout phrase. Determine roof versus ground from source evidence; do not guess an unresolved mounting location.

The existing output scope and TFS supplied-label-only rule still govern whether to include an array callout. When included, use one of the two phrases above. Panel geometry, counts, spacing, clearances and stroke weights remain governed by the production rules.
